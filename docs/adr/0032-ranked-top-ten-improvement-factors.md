# ADR-0032: Ranked top ten improvements in every initial assessment

**Status:** Accepted — active  
**Extends:** [ADR-0030](0030-substantive-initial-expert-reports.md).

## Context

The first long-report trial did not produce the ordered improvement priorities leadership needs. A narrative may explain problems well yet leave team leads uncertain about **what to fix first**.

## Decision

Every initial perspective report includes a clearly ranked **Top 10 improvement factors**, in descending order of **expected impact on real user/delivery outcomes and consequential risk**, with evidence/confidence, reason, specific action and a measure of improvement for each. Rank by expert judgment, including expected impact, urgency, leverage/dependencies and what the evidence actually establishes—**not** a weighted arithmetic score or method-compliance checklist. The order can differ under conventional, AI-assisted and AI-driven standards.

The target is **ten defensible items**, not ten invented faults. Valid actions include verified remedies, substantiated risks, high-value evidence discovery, and realistic sustained-improvement opportunities. Explicitly distinguish these from proven defects. When fewer than ten defensible actions exist, show fewer and **state why**, rather than padding the report with arbitrary recommendations or punishing excellent delivery.

The top ten belongs in the **initial** 5–10-page report, not an optional technical appendix or an agent SKILL.md generated prematurely.

## Consequences

Reports become actionable and priorities visible. The requirement preserves evidence integrity and avoids forcing a ten-item backlog on teams already delivering exceptionally well.
